Dwarf Galaxies and Dark Matter

Ultra-diffuse galaxies (UDGs) — extremely low-surface-brightness galaxies with sizes comparable to the Milky Way but stellar masses typical of dwarf galaxies — offer unique laboratories for studying dark matter. Their globular cluster (GC) populations carry rich information about the host dark-matter halo, providing an avenue to constrain the dark-matter distribution using photometric data alone. We explore this connection in the following project.

Constraining Dark Matter with Globular-Cluster Mass Segregation: NGC5846-UDG1

Liang, J., Jiang, F., Danieli, S., Benson, A., & Hopkins, P. (2024), The Astrophysical Journal, 963, 76. [ADS] [arXiv]

Motivation

The ultra-diffuse galaxy NGC5846-UDG1 hosts a surprisingly large population of ~50 globular clusters for its stellar mass of \(\sim 10^8\,M_\odot\). Two conflicting pictures emerge for its dark-matter content. On one hand, applying the empirical GC-abundance–halo-mass relation (Harris et al. 2017) yields an overly massive halo of \(\sim 10^{11}\,M_\odot\). On the other hand, the GC population exhibits striking radial mass segregation — more massive GCs lie closer to the galaxy centre — which is a hallmark of dynamical-friction-driven orbital decay. Since the timescale for orbital decay depends on the GC-to-host mass ratio \(m/M\), significant mass segregation requires that the host halo cannot be orders of magnitude more massive than the stellar component.

To resolve this tension, we develop a semi-analytical model of GC evolution that self-consistently tracks orbits, masses, and structures of GCs in a composite host potential. Combined with MCMC inference, this allows us to use photometric imaging data alone — without costly spectroscopic observations — to statistically constrain the dark-matter halo profile of UDG1.

Model

Orbital Evolution with Dynamical Friction

Each GC orbits in a composite host potential (dark matter + stars), with its trajectory governed by

\[ \ddot{\mathbf{r}} = -\nabla\Phi + \mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{DF}} \]

with \(\gamma(r) = -\mathrm{d}\ln\rho/\mathrm{d}\ln r\) the local logarithmic density slope, and

\[ b_{\min} = \max\!\left(l_{1/2},\; \frac{Gm}{V^2}\right) \tag{4} \]

where \(l_{1/2}\) is the half-mass radius of the GC. This treatment naturally captures the core-stalling effect: when a GC approaches a flat density core (\(\gamma \to 0\)), the Coulomb logarithm vanishes and orbital decay stalls. Unified Model of GC Mass-Size Evolution

Over 10 Gyr, GCs undergo both tidal stripping by the host potential and internal two-body relaxation (evaporation). Both processes drive mass loss and structural changes that in turn affect the dynamical friction strength. We model these coupled effects using a unified formalism.

Starting from the binding energy \(E \propto -G m^{5/3}\rho_{1/2}^{1/3}\), we differentiate and separate the tidal ("t") and relaxation ("r") contributions:

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}E_t + \mathrm{d}E_r}{E} = \frac{5}{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}m_t + \mathrm{d}m_r}{m} + \frac{1}{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{1/2}}{\rho_{1/2}} \tag{6} \]

We introduce coupling parameters \(f_t\) and \(f_r\) that relate the mass loss to the energy input for each process:

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}m_t}{m} = f_t\,\frac{\mathrm{d}E_t}{E}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}m_r}{m} = f_r\,\frac{\mathrm{d}E_r}{E} \tag{7, 8} \]

The tidal mass loss rate is computed as

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}m_t}{m} = -\alpha\,\xi_t\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\tau_{\mathrm{dyn}}} \tag{9} \]

where \(\xi_t = [m - m(l_t)]/m\) is the mass fraction beyond the tidal radius \(l_t\), \(\tau_{\mathrm{dyn}}\) is the host dynamical time,

\[ \tau_{\mathrm{dyn}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{16G\bar{\rho}(r)}} \tag{10} \]

and \(\alpha \approx 0.55\) is calibrated from N-body simulations. The tidal radius itself is

\[ l_t = r\left(\frac{m(l_t)/M(r)}{2 - \mathrm{d}\ln M/\mathrm{d}\ln r + V_t^2/V_{\mathrm{circ}}^2(r)}\right)^{1/3} \tag{11} \]

The evaporation rate from two-body relaxation is

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}m_r}{m} = -\xi_e\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\tau_r} \tag{12} \]

where \(\xi_e \approx 0.0074\) is the fraction of stars above the escape velocity, and \(\tau_r\) is the relaxation timescale:

\[ \tau_r \approx 0.142\;\mathrm{Gyr}\left(\frac{m}{10^4\,M_\odot}\right)\left(\frac{\rho_{1/2}}{10^{11}\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-3}}\right)^{-1/2} \tag{13} \]

Combining all these, we obtain a single equation governing the structural evolution:

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{1/2}}{\rho_{1/2}} = \left[\alpha\!\left(5 - \frac{3}{f_t}\right)\frac{\xi_t}{\tau_{\mathrm{dyn}}} + \left(5 - \frac{3}{f_r}\right)\frac{\xi_e}{\tau_r}\right]\mathrm{d}t \tag{14} \]

The tidal coupling parameter \(f_t\) is calibrated by matching the tidal evolutionary tracks of Peñarrubia et al. (2010) for collisionless systems with cored profiles:

\[ f_t = 0.77\,x^{0.19}, \quad x = m/m(0) \tag{16} \]

The relaxation coupling parameter is derived from the classical Hénon (1965) result:

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}E_r}{E} = -\zeta\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\tau_r}, \quad \zeta \approx 0.0926 \tag{17} \]

giving

\[ f_r = \xi_e / \zeta \approx 0.08 \tag{18} \]

Initial Conditions and Host Potential

The initial cluster mass function (ICMF) follows a power-law with exponential truncations at both ends:

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}N}{\mathrm{d}m} \propto m^\beta \exp\!\left(-\frac{m_{\min}}{m}\right)\exp\!\left(-\frac{m}{m_{\max}}\right) \tag{19} \]

with \(\beta = -2\), \(m_{\min} = 10^{5.5}\,M_\odot\), and \(m_{\max} = 10^8\,M_\odot\). Star clusters are described by the Elson, Fall & Freeman (1987, EFF) density profile:

\[ \rho(l) = \frac{\rho_0}{(1 + l^2/a^2)^\eta} \tag{20} \]

with outer slope \(-2\eta = -4\), and the scale radius linked to the half-mass radius by

\[ a = l_{1/2}\big/\!\left(2^{2/(2\eta-3)} - 1\right)^{1/2} \tag{21} \]

Initial sizes follow the observed size-mass relation of young star clusters: \(l_{1/2} = 2.55\;\mathrm{pc}\,(m/10^4\,M_\odot)^{0.24}\) (Brown & Gnedin 2021). The GC progenitors are born 10 Gyr ago with an isotropic Hernquist spatial distribution of scale radius \(r_0\) (a free parameter), and no initial mass segregation.

The host potential consists of UDG1's smooth stellar distribution and a dark-matter halo. The stellar profile is

\[ \rho_\star(r) = \frac{\rho_{0,\star}}{(1+x)(1+x^3)} \tag{23} \]

with \(x = r/r_s\), \(M_\star = 10^{8.3}\,M_\odot\), and \(r_s = 2\) kpc. For the dark-matter halo, we consider both the cuspy NFW profile:

\[ \rho_{\mathrm{NFW}}(r) = \frac{\rho_0}{x(1+x)^2} \tag{24} \]

and the cored Burkert profile:

\[ \rho_{\mathrm{Burkert}}(r) = \frac{\rho_0}{(1+x)(1+x^2)} \tag{25} \]

Initial velocities are sampled from the equilibrium distribution function computed via Eddington inversion:

\[ f(E) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{8}\,\pi^2}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{E}}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}\Psi}\right|_{\Psi=0} + \int_0^E \frac{\mathrm{d}\Psi}{\sqrt{E-\Psi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\rho}{\mathrm{d}\Psi^2}\right] \tag{26} \]

with the conditional velocity distribution at each radius:

\[ P(V|r) = \frac{4\pi V^2\,f(\Psi(r) - V^2/2)}{\rho(r)} \tag{27} \]

MCMC Parameter Inference

We use the emcee MCMC sampler to constrain three free parameters: halo mass \(M_h\), halo concentration \(c\), and the initial GC scale radius \(r_0\). The likelihood is constructed from the observed GC masses, half-mass radii, and projected galactocentric distances (Danieli et al. 2022), binned into three logarithmic mass bins. We parameterise the mass-segregation signal using the slopes \(\gamma_{ij}\) between bins and the relative GC number fractions \(f_i\).

Main Results

1. UDG1 is dark-matter poor with a low-concentration halo

For both NFW and Burkert halo assumptions, the posterior halo masses are low: the mode values are \(M_h = 10^{9.1}\,M_\odot\) (NFW) and \(10^{9.0}\,M_\odot\) (Burkert). With a stellar mass of \(\sim 10^{8}\,M_\odot\), this puts UDG1 in the dark-matter-poor regime with \(M_\star/M_h \sim 0.1\), far above the stellar-to-halo-mass relation for normal galaxies. The halo concentrations are also below the cosmological average, consistent with UDG formation via repeated supernovae feedback that puffs up both the halo and the stellar distribution.

2. The model reproduces the observed GC statistics

Model realizations with the best-fit parameters reproduce the observed radial mass segregation, GC mass distribution, size distribution, and size-mass relation simultaneously. The initially broad mass function evolves towards the observed narrow one: low-mass clusters dissolve, intermediate-mass clusters expand, and massive clusters remain intact and sink inward via dynamical friction.

3. UDG1 is an outlier to galaxy–halo scaling relations

With our halo-mass estimates, UDG1 deviates from several empirical scaling relations by \(\sim 2\text{–}3\sigma\): it is a dramatic outlier to the GC-abundance–halo-mass relation (which would predict \(M_{\mathrm{vir}} \sim 10^{11}\,M_\odot\) for 50 GCs), it lies above the stellar-mass–halo-mass relation, and its NFW concentration is far below the cosmological average. This warns against naively using the GC-abundance relation to estimate halo masses for UDGs.

4. Cuspy vs. cored halos and nuclear star cluster formation

A key observational discriminant between cuspy and cored halos is the formation of nuclear star clusters (NSCs) from GC coalescence at the galaxy centre. In cuspy NFW halos, massive GCs can sink completely to form an NSC, whereas in cored halos, core-stalling prevents this. Since UDG1 appears non-nucleated, our results favour a cored, low-mass halo — consistent with core formation via supernovae-driven potential fluctuations.

5. Velocity dispersion and comparison with kinematics

Our model predicts that the GC velocity dispersion is significantly lower than that of the smooth stellar background — another signature of dynamical friction. The predicted GC dispersion of \(\sim 8\) km/s at \(R \approx 1.8\) kpc agrees well with the observed value of \(\sigma = 9.4^{+7.0}_{-5.4}\) km/s from Müller et al. (2020).

Conclusions

We have demonstrated that the radial mass segregation of globular clusters in ultra-diffuse galaxies can be used to constrain the dark-matter distribution using photometric data alone. Our semi-analytical model self-consistently evolves GC orbits, masses, and internal structures under dynamical friction, tidal stripping, and two-body relaxation. Applied to NGC5846-UDG1, we find that the galaxy inhabits a low-mass (\(M_h \sim 10^9\,M_\odot\)), low-concentration dark-matter halo, irrespective of whether the profile is cuspy (NFW) or cored (Burkert). UDG1 is an outlier to multiple galaxy–halo scaling relations by \(\sim 2\text{–}3\sigma\), warning against naively using the GC-abundance–halo-mass relation for UDGs. The difference between cuspy and cored profiles manifests most clearly in nuclear star cluster formation: cuspy halos form massive NSCs via GC coalescence, while cored halos do not — suggesting that non-nucleated UDGs like UDG1 preferentially inhabit cored halos, consistent with the supernovae-driven core formation scenario.
