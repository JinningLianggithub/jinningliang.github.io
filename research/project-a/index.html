--- layout: default title: Simulating AGN Feedback --- # Simulating AGN Feedback --- ## Introduction Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) exert powerful influence on their host galaxies. These feedback processes regulate star formation, impact gas inflows, and shape the co-evolution of galaxies and supermassive black holes (SMBHs). Most large-volume cosmological simulations treat AGN feedback using simplified prescriptions with **constant coupling efficiency**—the fraction of AGN luminosity converted to kinetic energy. However, observations and small-scale accretion-disc models (especially UV line-driven wind models) suggest that the coupling efficiency **should depend strongly on the black hole accretion rate**, typically following a power law. In this project, I introduce a **new AGN feedback model** where the coupling efficiency varies with the **Eddington ratio**, motivated by UV line-driven wind theory, and study its impact on disc galaxy evolution. --- ## Simulation Framework Our simulations use the **SWIFT** hydrodynamic code with the **COLIBRE** galaxy formation model. We construct idealised Milky Way-like galaxies including: - A static dark matter halo - Stellar disc - Cold gas disc - Hot circumgalactic medium (CGM) - A central SMBH ### Key components of the modelling: - **Thermal AGN feedback**, representing shock-heated disc winds - **Variable coupling efficiency:** \[ \eta = \min(N_\eta \, \dot{m}^{\alpha_\eta}, 1) \] where - \( \dot{m} \) = Eddington ratio - \( N_\eta, \alpha_\eta \) = free parameters motivated by UV line-driven wind models - **BH growth regulated by Bondi accretion with turbulence/vorticity corrections** - **Self-consistent star formation, cooling, SN feedback, and chemical enrichment** --- ## Figure Placeholder (attach your own) **Figure 1:** *Schematic illustration of the simulation setup*, including the stellar disc, gas disc, hot CGM, and the location of the SMBH. --- ## Feedback Model: Variable Coupling Efficiency UV line-driven wind models predict that the kinetic luminosity of AGN winds scales with the Eddington ratio as: \[ L_{\text{kin}} / L_{\text{bol}} \propto \dot{m}^{2.6} \] This motivates replacing the traditional constant \(\eta\) with a physically motivated one that evolves with accretion state. We explore: - **Fiducial variable model:** \[ \eta \propto \dot{m}^{2.6} \] - **Constant-efficiency control model:** \(\eta = 0.05\) - **Variations in slope and normalization:** \(\alpha_\eta = 0.5, 1.5, 3.0\), \(N_\eta = 10, 300, 3000\) These tests reveal how sensitive AGN wind feedback is to the underlying physics of SMBH accretion. --- ## Figure Placeholder **Figure 2:** *Coupling efficiency vs. Eddington ratio* for different values of \(N_\eta\) and \(\alpha_\eta\). --- ## Results ### 1. **Black Hole Growth Changes Dramatically** Lower coupling efficiency -> weaker AGN feedback -> **much faster SMBH growth**. This is the most prominent outcome of the study. - High-\eta models: AGN quickly self-regulates -> low accretion - Low-\eta models: sustained high Eddington ratios -> rapid mass growth #### Figure Placeholder **Figure 2:** *BH accretion rate and mass growth history* for constant vs. variable efficiency. --- ### 2. **Outflow Strength Depends Strongly on Efficiency** - High-\eta: short, powerful bursts; strong hot bubbles - Low-\eta: weaker but long-lasting outflows These behaviours directly track the evolution of the coupling efficiency. #### Figure Placeholder **Figure 3:** *AGN-driven outflow structure* (density/temperature maps comparing high-\eta and low-\eta runs). --- ### 3. **Galaxy Properties Remain Relatively Stable** The global galaxy structure-gas mass, star formation rate, disc morphology-changes only mildly across models.
